Baby oral care and teething — cleaning gums and first teeth

Updated October 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Before teeth appear, wipe your baby’s gums with a clean, damp cloth once or twice a day. Once the first tooth comes through — usually around 6 months — brush twice a day with a soft baby toothbrush and a smear of fluoride toothpaste the size of a grain of rice. Teething can cause drooling, chewing and fussiness, but not a high fever. Book a first dental visit by the first birthday.

Good habits start before the first tooth appears. Cleaning your baby’s mouth gets them used to the routine and keeps gums healthy.

Before the first tooth

  • Once or twice a day, wrap a clean, damp cloth or gauze around your finger and gently wipe the gums and tongue.
  • A good time is after the last feed of the day.

After the first tooth

  • Brush twice a day, including last thing at night.
  • Use a soft, small-headed baby toothbrush.
  • Use a smear of fluoride toothpaste — about the size of a grain of rice — until age 3, then a pea-sized amount until age 6.
  • Encourage your baby to spit out the excess; there is no need to rinse with water afterwards.
  • Lift the lip to check for white or brown spots along the gum line — an early sign of decay.

Protecting teeth

  • Avoid putting your baby to bed with a bottle of milk or juice.
  • Start moving from bottle to cup from around 6 months, aiming to stop bottles by about 12–18 months.
  • Avoid sugary drinks and snacks. Water and milk are the best drinks.

Signs of teething

  • Drooling and chewing on fingers or toys;
  • red, swollen gums or a flushed cheek;
  • rubbing the ear on the same side;
  • fussiness and disturbed sleep.

Many babies show few signs at all.

Safe teething relief

  • A chilled (not frozen) teething ring or clean cold flannel to chew on.
  • Rub the gums gently with a clean finger.
  • Hard, sugar-free finger foods for babies over 6 months — always supervised.
  • If your baby is in pain, ask a pharmacist or doctor about an age-appropriate pain reliever.

Avoid amber teething necklaces or bracelets (choking and strangulation risk) and teething gels containing benzocaine or lidocaine.

First dental visit

Take your baby to a dentist by their first birthday, or within six months of the first tooth. Early visits are quick and help your child get used to the dentist.

Ticking “oral care” in your tracker each day makes it easier to build the routine — especially when several people share the care.

Frequently asked questions

When do babies get their first tooth?＋

Usually between 4 and 12 months, most often around 6 months. The bottom front teeth usually come first. A few babies are born with a tooth, and some get their first tooth after their first birthday.

Can I use fluoride toothpaste for a baby?＋

Yes. For children under 3, health bodies recommend a smear (rice-grain amount) of fluoride toothpaste. From 3 to 6 years, use a pea-sized amount.

Does teething cause fever or diarrhoea?＋

Teething may cause a slightly raised temperature, but not a true fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or more. If your baby has a fever or diarrhoea, look for another cause.

Medical disclaimer

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Every baby is different. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. Seek urgent help if your baby has trouble breathing, is unusually sleepy or floppy, or is under 3 months old with a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher.
