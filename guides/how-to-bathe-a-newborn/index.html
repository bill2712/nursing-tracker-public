How to bathe a newborn — step by step

Updated October 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Newborns only need a bath 2–3 times a week; on other days, wash the face, neck, hands and diaper area. Give sponge baths until the umbilical cord stump falls off. Use about 5–10 cm (2–4 in) of water at around 37–38°C (98–100°F), gather everything before you start, keep one hand on your baby, and never leave them alone in water — not even for a second.

Bath time can be a lovely way to bond — and it often helps babies settle before sleep. It also feels nerve-racking the first few times. Preparation makes all the difference.

How often?

Two or three baths a week are plenty in the first year. Newborn skin is delicate, and bathing too often can dry it out. In between, “top and tail”: wipe the face, neck creases and hands with warm water, and clean the diaper area at every change.

Before the cord falls off: sponge baths

  1. Lay your baby on a towel on a safe, flat surface in a warm room.
  2. Keep them wrapped in a towel and uncover one area at a time.
  3. Wipe the eyes from the inside corner outwards with cotton wool dipped in cooled boiled water, using a fresh piece for each eye.
  4. Wash the face, behind the ears and the neck creases, then the body, and the diaper area last.
  5. Keep the cord stump clean and dry. Fold the diaper below it.

Step-by-step bath

Get ready first

  • A warm room, a baby bath or clean sink, and 5–10 cm (2–4 in) of water.
  • Water at about 37–38°C (98–100°F). Put cold water in first, then hot, and mix well. Check with a thermometer or your elbow.
  • A towel, a clean diaper, clothes and any cleanser within reach.

In the bath

  1. Lower your baby gently, feet first, supporting the head and neck with one arm and holding the far arm.
  2. Keep one hand on your baby at all times.
  3. Wash the face with a soft cloth, then the body, creases and diaper area.
  4. Wash the hair last so your baby does not get cold, pouring water from front to back.
  5. A newborn bath only needs about 5–10 minutes.

After the bath

Lift your baby out, wrap them in a towel straight away and pat dry — especially the creases in the neck, armpits and groin. Dress them quickly to keep them warm.

Safety rules

  • Never leave your baby alone in the bath or with an older child in charge, even for a moment. Babies can drown in a few centimetres of water. If you must leave, take your baby with you.
  • Do not top up with hot water while your baby is in the bath.
  • Bath seats are not safety devices.
  • Keep electrical appliances away from water.

Skin care tips

  • Plain water is fine for the first month. After that use only a little mild, unscented cleanser.
  • Do not use cotton buds inside the ears or nose.
  • Dry, flaky skin in the first weeks is common and usually settles on its own.
  • Cradle cap (yellow, greasy scales on the scalp) is harmless. Massage in a little baby oil, then brush gently.

If you notice a rash that does not fade when you press a glass on it, or your baby seems unwell, seek medical help straight away.

Frequently asked questions

When can I give my baby their first bath?＋

Many hospitals now suggest waiting at least 24 hours after birth. Until the cord stump falls off (usually 1–3 weeks), give sponge baths or “top and tail” washes.

What temperature should a baby’s bath be?＋

Around 37–38°C (98–100°F) — warm, not hot. Use a bath thermometer or test with your elbow, and mix the water well to avoid hot spots.

Do I need baby soap or shampoo?＋

Plain water is enough for the first weeks. After that, use a small amount of mild, fragrance-free baby cleanser if you like.

Medical disclaimer

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Every baby is different. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. Seek urgent help if your baby has trouble breathing, is unusually sleepy or floppy, or is under 3 months old with a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher.
