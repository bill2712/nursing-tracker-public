Tummy time — why it matters and how to make it fun

Updated October 1, 2026 · 2 min read

Tummy time is supervised time on the tummy while your baby is awake. Start from the first days with 1–2 minutes a few times a day and build up gradually. The WHO recommends at least 30 minutes a day, spread through the day, for babies who are not yet mobile. It strengthens the neck, shoulders and back, supports rolling and crawling, and helps prevent flat spots on the head.

Because babies sleep on their backs for safety, they need some time on their tummies while awake to build strength. Tummy time is the foundation for lifting the head, rolling, sitting and crawling.

Why it matters

  • Builds strength in the neck, shoulders, arms and back.
  • Helps motor development: pushing up, rolling, reaching and, later, crawling.
  • Reduces flat spots on the back of the head (positional plagiocephaly).
  • Gives a new view of the world — babies see and reach for things differently on their tummy.

How much and how often

  • Newborns: 1–2 minutes, 2–3 times a day, ideally when your baby is alert and content.
  • Build up gradually. Add a little time each day.
  • Goal: the WHO recommends babies who are not yet crawling get at least 30 minutes a day on their tummy, spread across short sessions.

Tummy time does not have to happen on the floor. These all count:

  • Chest to chest — lie back and place your baby on your chest. Your face is the best motivation.
  • Across your lap — lay your baby across your knees and gently rub their back.
  • Tummy-down carry — carry your baby facing down along your forearm, supporting the chest and head.

Ideas to make it fun

  • Get down on the floor at eye level and talk, sing or make faces.
  • Place a baby-safe mirror or high-contrast cards in front of them.
  • Put a rolled towel under the chest and armpits for a little support.
  • Offer toys just out of reach to encourage reaching and pivoting.
  • Try different surfaces: a play mat, a blanket on the floor, a firm cushion.

If your baby dislikes it

Many babies protest at first. Keep sessions short and frequent, stop before they get very upset, and try again later. Avoid tummy time straight after a feed. As your baby gets stronger, they usually start to enjoy it.

Safety

  • Only on a firm, flat surface and while your baby is awake and supervised.
  • Never leave your baby on a bed, sofa or raised surface.
  • Always put your baby on their back to sleep.

When to ask for advice

Talk to your health visitor or doctor if your baby always turns their head to one side, has a noticeable flat spot, or is not lifting their head during tummy time by about 3 months.

Frequently asked questions

When should tummy time start?＋

From the first days at home, as long as your baby is awake and you are watching. Lying on your chest counts.

My baby cries during tummy time. What can I do?＋

Keep sessions short, try lying chest to chest, get down at eye level, use a mirror or a rolled towel under the chest, and try after a diaper change rather than straight after a feed.

Can my baby sleep on their tummy?＋

No. Tummy time is for awake, supervised play only. Always put your baby on their back to sleep.

Medical disclaimer

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Every baby is different. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. Seek urgent help if your baby has trouble breathing, is unusually sleepy or floppy, or is under 3 months old with a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher.
