Newborn

Newborn milestones

Welcome to the world! In the first weeks your baby is adjusting to life outside the womb, learning to feed and getting to know your voice, smell and face.

✨ Skills to look for🌧️ Common challenges🏃 Play & interaction ideas❤️ Care tips ⚠️ Talk to a doctor if

✨ Skills to look for

  • Rooting reflex

    When you stroke their cheek, your baby turns towards your touch and opens their mouth, looking for the breast or bottle.

  • Startle (Moro) reflex

    A loud noise or a feeling of falling makes your baby fling their arms out and then pull them back in.

  • Grasp reflex

    Your baby grips a finger tightly when you touch their palm.

  • Sees faces up close

    Newborns see best about 20–30 cm (8–12 in) away — roughly the distance to your face during a feed.

  • Knows your voice

    Your baby may calm or turn towards the familiar voices they heard in the womb.

🌧️ Common challenges

  • Lots of crying

    Crying is your baby’s only way to communicate hunger, a wet diaper, being too hot or cold, or needing comfort.

  • Irregular sleep

    Day–night confusion is common. Most newborns wake every 2–4 hours to feed.

  • Newborn jaundice

    Yellow skin or eyes in the first days is common, but tell your midwife or doctor so they can check whether treatment is needed.

🏃 Play & interaction ideas

  • Tummy time

    While awake and supervised, lay your baby on your chest or a firm flat surface for 1–2 minutes a few times a day to strengthen neck muscles.

  • High-contrast pictures

    Hold black-and-white cards 20–30 cm from your baby’s face and move them slowly to encourage tracking.

  • Skin-to-skin contact

    Holding your baby in just a diaper against your bare chest helps steady their breathing, heart rate and temperature, and supports bonding and breastfeeding.

  • Gentle massage

    After a bath, gently stroke your baby’s arms, legs and back to help them relax.

❤️ Care tips

  • Umbilical cord care

    Keep the stump clean and dry and fold the diaper below it. It usually falls off within 1–3 weeks. Watch for redness, swelling or a bad smell.

  • Sponge baths first

    Until the cord falls off, a sponge bath is easiest. Keep the water around 37–38°C (98–100°F), the room warm and baths short.

  • Count wet diapers

    From about day 5–6, expect at least 6 heavy wet diapers in 24 hours — a good sign your baby is getting enough milk.

  • Safe sleep

    Always put your baby to sleep on their back, on a firm flat mattress with no pillows, bumpers, blankets or soft toys, in your room.

  • Burping

    Burp your baby during and after feeds to reduce wind and spit-up.

⚠️ Talk to a doctor if

  • Feeding poorly, or fewer than 6 wet diapers a day after the first week
  • A temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher — seek medical help urgently
  • Yellow skin that spreads or deepens, or appears in the first 24 hours
  • Being very hard to wake, floppy, or breathing fast or with effort

Every baby develops at their own pace. Milestones are a guide, not a test — most babies reach them within a range of weeks or months. Babies born early often reach milestones based on their corrected age.
