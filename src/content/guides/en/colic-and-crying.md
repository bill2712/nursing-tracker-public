Crying is how babies communicate. Even when every need has been met, many young babies have periods of crying that are hard to soothe — often in the late afternoon and evening.

## How much crying is normal?

Crying tends to increase from about 2 weeks, peak at around **6–8 weeks**, and settle by **3–4 months**. During the peak, 2–3 hours of crying spread over a day is common.

**Colic** is the name often used when a healthy, feeding, growing baby cries for more than 3 hours a day, on more than 3 days a week, for at least 3 weeks. Babies with colic may clench their fists, pull up their legs and go red in the face.

## First, check the basics

- Hungry? Offer a feed.
- Wet or dirty diaper?
- Too hot or cold? Feel the back of the neck or the tummy.
- Wind? Try burping.
- Tired or overstimulated? Move somewhere quiet and dim.
- Something uncomfortable — a hair wrapped around a toe, a scratchy label?

## Ways to soothe a crying baby

Different things work for different babies, and what works one day may not the next.

- **Hold your baby close**, skin to skin, or in a sling.
- **Swaddle** with a thin wrap, hips loose — but stop as soon as your baby shows signs of rolling.
- **Gentle, rhythmic motion:** rocking, swaying, walking, or a pram ride.
- **Shushing or white noise** — a steady “shhh”, a fan or a white-noise app.
- **Sucking** on a dummy (pacifier) or a clean finger.
- **A warm bath** or gentle tummy massage.
- **A change of scene:** step outside or into another room.

Holding your baby on their side or tummy across your arm can help while they are awake, but always put them down **on their back to sleep**.

## Look after yourself

Persistent crying is exhausting and can make anyone feel frustrated or upset. That is normal.

- If you feel overwhelmed, **put your baby down safely** on their back in the crib, close the door and take a few minutes to breathe. Check on them every 5–10 minutes.
- Share the load — take turns with a partner, family member or friend.
- Talk to your health visitor or doctor about how you are feeling.

**Never shake a baby.** Shaking can cause serious brain injury or death.

## When to get medical help

Contact a doctor urgently if your baby:

- has a temperature of **38°C (100.4°F) or higher and is under 3 months**;
- has a **high-pitched, weak or unusual cry**;
- is **floppy, very sleepy** or hard to wake;
- vomits repeatedly, has green vomit, or blood in their stool;
- is not feeding, or has fewer wet diapers;
- has breathing difficulties or a rash that does not fade under a glass.

Also see a doctor if crying is suddenly worse, lasts beyond 4 months, or you simply feel something is wrong. You know your baby best.

Tracking crying spells alongside feeds and sleep can reveal patterns — and give your doctor useful information.
