Fever is one of the most common reasons parents seek medical advice. Most fevers are caused by viral infections and go away on their own, but in young babies a fever can be the first sign of a serious infection.

> **If your baby is under 3 months old and has a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher, get medical help straight away**, even if they seem otherwise well.

## What counts as a fever?

- Normal: about **36.4–37.5°C (97.5–99.5°F)**.
- Fever: **38°C (100.4°F) or higher**.

A baby can also be seriously unwell without a fever, so always look at the whole picture.

## How to take your baby’s temperature

- Use a **digital thermometer**.
- **Armpit:** place the tip in the middle of the armpit, hold the arm against the body and wait for the beep. This is the usual method at home for young babies.
- **Rectal** readings are most accurate in infants, but only use this method if a health professional has shown you how.
- **Ear** thermometers are less reliable under 6 months. **Forehead strips** and touching the skin are not accurate.

## When to get urgent help

Call emergency services or go to the emergency department if your baby:

- has **difficulty breathing** — fast breathing, grunting, or the skin pulling in between the ribs;
- has **blue, grey or very pale** skin or lips;
- has a **rash that does not fade** when you press a glass against it;
- is **floppy, unresponsive** or very hard to wake;
- has a **seizure** (fit);
- has a **bulging soft spot** on the head, or a stiff neck;
- is **under 3 months with a temperature of 38°C or more**.

## When to call a doctor the same day

- Age 3–6 months with a temperature of **39°C (102.2°F) or higher**.
- A fever lasting more than 24 hours in a baby under 2, or more than 5 days at any age.
- **Signs of dehydration:** fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, no tears, sunken soft spot.
- Not feeding, vomiting repeatedly, or crying in a way that seems unusual.
- You are worried — trust your instincts.

## Keeping your baby comfortable

- **Offer frequent feeds** of breast milk or formula. Babies over 6 months can also have sips of water.
- **Dress them lightly** and keep the room comfortably cool. Do not bundle them up.
- **Do not sponge** your baby with cold water or alcohol.
- Check on them regularly, including during the night.
- Ask a pharmacist or doctor about medicine. Dosing for babies depends on age and weight.

## Keeping a record

Writing down temperatures, times and any medicine given helps you avoid double doses and gives your doctor a clear picture. A tracker’s notes field is handy for this.
