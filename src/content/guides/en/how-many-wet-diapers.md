Diapers are one of the best ways to tell whether a young baby is getting enough milk. That is why midwives and nurses often ask parents to count them in the first weeks.

## Wet diapers by day

| Baby’s age | Wet diapers in 24 h | Stools in 24 h |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | 1 or more | 1 or more, black and sticky (meconium) |
| Day 2 | 2 or more | 1 or more, black or dark green |
| Day 3 | 3 or more | 2 or more, green-brown (changing) |
| Day 4 | 4 or more | 2 or more, turning yellow |
| Day 5–6 onwards | **6 or more, heavy** | usually 2 or more, yellow |

A “heavy” wet diaper holds about 45 ml (3 tablespoons) of liquid. Modern disposables are very absorbent, so it can help to compare with a dry diaper, or pour 45 ml of water into one to feel the weight.

## Normal poop colours

- **Meconium (day 1–2):** black or dark green, thick and sticky.
- **Transitional (day 3–4):** green to brown, looser.
- **Breastfed babies:** mustard yellow, loose, often seedy or curdy.
- **Formula-fed babies:** pale yellow to tan, thicker, like peanut butter, with a stronger smell.
- **Green** stool is common and usually fine, especially if your baby is otherwise well.

Once solids start, colour and texture change with what your baby eats.

## How often is normal?

In the first six weeks, breastfed babies usually poop at least 2–3 times a day, sometimes after every feed. After that, frequency varies widely. Formula-fed babies tend to poop less often, and the stools are firmer.

Constipation is about **hard, dry stools that are difficult to pass**, not about how often your baby goes. Straining and going red in the face while passing soft stool is normal.

## Signs of dehydration

Contact a doctor promptly if your baby has:

- fewer wet diapers than expected, or none for 6–8 hours;
- dark yellow, strong-smelling urine;
- a dry mouth or no tears when crying;
- a sunken soft spot (fontanelle) on the top of the head;
- unusual sleepiness, floppiness or irritability.

## Stools that need checking

- **Pale, white, grey or chalky** stools — can be a sign of a liver problem. Tell your doctor the same day.
- **Black** stools after the first few days, or **red blood** in the diaper.
- Very watery stools many times a day, especially with fever or vomiting.

## Tracking diapers

Counting diapers on little sleep is hard. Logging each change — wet, dirty or both, and optionally the urine volume — lets you see today’s total at a glance. If you are worried, you will have clear numbers to share with your midwife or doctor.
