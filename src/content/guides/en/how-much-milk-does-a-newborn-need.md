Every baby is different, and appetite changes from day to day. The numbers below are typical ranges, not targets to hit at every feed. Your baby’s hunger cues, wet diapers and weight gain matter more than any single number.

## A newborn’s tummy is tiny

On the first day a newborn’s stomach holds only about 5–7 ml — roughly a teaspoon. It grows quickly over the first week, which is why feeds are small and frequent at first. Colostrum, the thick early breast milk, comes in exactly these small amounts and is packed with antibodies.

## Typical feeding amounts by age

These are common ranges for formula or expressed breast milk given by bottle.

| Age | Per feed | Feeds in 24 h | Total per day |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1–2 | 15–30 ml (½–1 oz) | 8–12 | 120–300 ml |
| Day 3–7 | 30–90 ml (1–3 oz) | 8–12 | 300–600 ml |
| 2–4 weeks | 60–120 ml (2–4 oz) | 7–9 | 450–750 ml |
| 1–2 months | 90–150 ml (3–5 oz) | 6–8 | 600–900 ml |
| 3–5 months | 120–210 ml (4–7 oz) | 5–6 | 700–1,000 ml |
| 6–12 months | 180–240 ml (6–8 oz) | 3–5, plus solids | about 500–700 ml |

## The weight-based rule of thumb

For formula-fed babies under about 6 months, a widely used guide is **150 ml per kilogram of body weight per day** (about 2.5 oz per pound), with a normal range of roughly 120–180 ml/kg.

For example, a 4 kg baby would drink around 600 ml a day; divided over 8 feeds that is about 75 ml per feed. Most babies never need more than about **1,000 ml (32 oz) a day**. You can try our [formula amount calculator](../../tools/formula-calculator/) to work this out for your baby.

## Breastfed babies

You cannot see how much a breastfed baby drinks, and you do not need to. Breastfed babies regulate their own intake when fed responsively:

- Feed whenever your baby shows hunger cues, usually 8–12 times in 24 hours.
- Let your baby finish the first breast before offering the second.
- Expect “cluster feeding” — several feeds close together, often in the evening. It is normal and helps build your supply.

## Hunger and fullness cues

**Early hunger signs:** stirring, turning the head and opening the mouth (rooting), sucking on hands, lip smacking. Crying is a late sign — it is easier to feed a baby before they get upset.

**Fullness signs:** sucking slows down, turning away, closing the lips, relaxed open hands, falling asleep.

With a bottle, try **paced feeding**: hold the bottle more horizontally, let your baby draw milk in, and pause every few minutes. It helps babies notice when they are full and reduces overfeeding.

## Signs your baby is getting enough

- At least **6 heavy wet diapers** a day from about day 5–6 (see our [wet diaper guide](../how-many-wet-diapers/)).
- Regular stools, which turn yellow by around day 5 for breastfed babies.
- Back to birth weight by about **10–14 days** (losing up to 7–10% in the first days is common), then steady gain.
- Alert periods and contentment after most feeds.

## When to get help

Contact your midwife, health visitor or doctor if your baby:

- has fewer wet diapers than expected, dark urine or dry lips;
- is very sleepy and hard to wake for feeds;
- has not regained their birth weight by 2 weeks;
- often vomits forcefully, or seems in pain during feeds.

Tracking feeds and diapers for a few days is the easiest way to answer the questions a professional will ask.
