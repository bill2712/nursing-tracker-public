Expressing (pumping) breast milk lets someone else feed your baby, helps you build or maintain supply, and keeps milk available when you are away. Safe storage keeps the milk’s nutrients and protects your baby from germs.

## Storage times at a glance

| Where | Temperature | Freshly expressed | Thawed milk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Room | up to 25°C (77°F) | up to 4 hours | 1–2 hours |
| Fridge | 4°C (39°F) or colder | up to 4 days | up to 24 hours |
| Freezer | −18°C (0°F) or colder | best within 6 months; up to 12 months | never refreeze |

If you are unsure how long milk has been stored, it is safest to throw it away.

## Storing milk safely

- **Wash your hands** before pumping and handling milk.
- Use clean containers made for breast milk: storage bags or bottles with tight lids.
- **Label each container** with the date (and time) it was expressed. Use the oldest milk first.
- Store small amounts — 60–120 ml (2–4 oz) — to avoid wasting milk.
- Leave space at the top of containers you freeze; milk expands.
- Keep milk at the back of the fridge or freezer, not in the door.

For travel, an insulated cooler with ice packs keeps fresh milk safe for up to 24 hours.

## Thawing and warming

1. Thaw frozen milk in the fridge overnight, or hold the container under warm running water or in a bowl of warm water.
2. Swirl gently to mix the fat that separates. Do not shake hard.
3. Warm the bottle in warm water if your baby prefers it warm. Test a few drops on your inner wrist — it should feel lukewarm, not hot.

**Never use a microwave.** It heats unevenly and can create hot spots that burn a baby’s mouth.

Once thawed, use the milk within 24 hours (if kept in the fridge) and **do not refreeze it**.

## Tips for pumping

- **Pump about as often as your baby feeds** if you are exclusively pumping — usually 8–10 times in 24 hours in the early weeks, including once at night.
- Use the right flange size. Pumping should not hurt; pain or rubbing often means the size is wrong.
- Relax and think about your baby — looking at a photo or smelling their clothes helps milk flow.
- **Hand expression** is useful in the first days for collecting colostrum, which comes in small amounts.
- Clean all pump parts that touch milk after every use, and let them air dry.
