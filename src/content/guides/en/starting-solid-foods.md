Starting solid food — also called weaning or complementary feeding — is an exciting step. At first it is mostly about learning to eat: tastes, textures and the skill of moving food around the mouth. Breast milk or formula remains your baby’s main food until about 12 months.

## When is my baby ready?

Around **6 months**, most babies show all three of these signs:

1. They can **sit up with support** and hold their head steady.
2. They can **coordinate eyes, hands and mouth** — look at food, pick it up and bring it to their mouth.
3. They can **swallow food** rather than pushing it back out with the tongue.

Chewing fists, waking more at night or wanting extra milk are *not* reliable signs of readiness. Babies born early may be ready later; ask your health professional.

## Good first foods

At around 6 months, babies’ iron stores start to run low, so include **iron-rich foods** every day:

- iron-fortified baby cereal;
- well-cooked meat, chicken or fish, finely minced or flaked;
- beans, lentils and tofu;
- eggs, cooked until firm.

Add **vegetables and fruit** — for example soft-cooked carrot, broccoli, sweet potato, avocado or banana. Offering vegetables often, including bitter ones, helps babies learn to like them.

## Textures: purées, mashed or finger foods?

Both spoon-feeding and **baby-led weaning** (letting babies feed themselves soft finger foods) are fine, and many families do a mix.

- Start with smooth or mashed textures, or soft finger-sized pieces that squash easily between your fingers.
- Move to lumpier and chopped textures within a few weeks. Babies who stay on smooth purées for too long can find lumps harder later.
- By around 12 months, most babies can eat chopped family food.

## Introducing allergens

Current advice is **not to delay** common allergens. Introduce them one at a time from around 6 months, in an age-appropriate form:

- well-cooked egg;
- smooth peanut butter thinned with water or mixed into food (never whole nuts);
- dairy such as yoghurt, wheat, fish, soy and sesame.

Once a food is tolerated, keep offering it regularly. If your baby has severe eczema or a known food allergy, talk to your doctor before introducing allergens.

## Foods to avoid

- **Honey** before 12 months (risk of infant botulism).
- **Whole nuts and whole grapes**, popcorn and hard raw vegetables — choking hazards. Cut grapes and cherry tomatoes into quarters lengthways.
- **Added salt and sugar.** Do not add salt to your baby’s food or use stock cubes.
- **Cow’s milk as a main drink** before 12 months (it is fine in cooking).
- Raw or lightly cooked eggs that are not from a safe source, unpasteurised cheese, and raw shellfish.
- Tea, coffee and sugary or fizzy drinks.

## Drinks

Offer **sips of water** from an open or free-flow cup with meals from 6 months. Breast milk or formula continues as the main drink.

## Safety at mealtimes

- Always stay with your baby while they eat.
- Sit them upright in a high chair, never reclined.
- Learn **infant first aid**, including what to do if a baby chokes.

## How much should my baby eat?

Start with one meal a day and a few teaspoons, building to three meals a day by around 7–9 months, plus snacks from about 12 months. Let your baby decide how much to eat — turning away and closing the mouth mean “I’m done”.

Recording new foods and any reactions is easy in a tracker: log the food, the amount and a note if you notice a rash or tummy upset.
