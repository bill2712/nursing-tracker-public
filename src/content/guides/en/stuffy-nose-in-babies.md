A blocked nose is one of the most common reasons young babies struggle to feed and sleep. Most colds are caused by viruses and get better on their own within 7–10 days.

## Why babies get stuffy

- **Narrow nasal passages** — newborns often sound snuffly even when well.
- **Colds** — babies can have 6–8 colds a year, more if they attend childcare.
- **Dry air**, especially with heating on.
- Less often, allergies or irritants such as smoke.

## How to clear a baby’s nose

You will need saline (salt water) drops or spray made for babies, and a bulb syringe or nasal aspirator.

1. Lay your baby on their back, with their head slightly tilted back.
2. Put **1–2 saline drops in each nostril**. Wait about 30–60 seconds to loosen the mucus.
3. **Squeeze the bulb first**, gently place the tip just inside the nostril, then release slowly. With an aspirator, follow the instructions.
4. Wipe away the mucus, clean the device with warm soapy water, and let it dry.

Saline drops on their own can be enough. Use suction gently, mostly **before feeds and sleep**.

## Other things that help

- **Keep up feeds.** Offer smaller, more frequent feeds if your baby is struggling to breathe while feeding.
- **Cool-mist humidifier** in the room, cleaned daily — or sit in a steamy bathroom for a few minutes (never leave your baby near hot water).
- **Hold your baby upright** while they are awake.
- Always put your baby to sleep on their back on a flat surface. Do not raise the mattress or use pillows.

## What to avoid

- **Cough and cold medicines** — many are not recommended for children under 2 (and in some countries under 6) because of side effects.
- **Vapour rubs, essential oils** and menthol products on young babies.
- **Cotton buds** inside the nose.

## When to see a doctor

Get **urgent help** if your baby:

- is breathing fast, the skin between the ribs or at the neck pulls in with each breath, or they are grunting;
- has blue or grey lips or face;
- has pauses in breathing.

Contact a doctor **the same day** if your baby:

- is **under 3 months and has a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher**;
- is feeding much less, or has fewer wet diapers;
- seems to have ear pain, or the symptoms last more than 10 days or get worse after improving.

Noting nose-clearing times in a tracker can help you see whether symptoms are getting better over a few days.
