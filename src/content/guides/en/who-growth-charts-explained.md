At every check-up your baby is weighed and measured, and the results are plotted on a growth chart. The chart is a powerful tool — but it is easy to over-read a single dot.

## What is a percentile?

The curved lines on a growth chart are **percentiles** (also called centiles). Each one shows what percentage of healthy children are below that measurement at a given age.

- **50th percentile:** the middle — half of babies are smaller, half are larger.
- **10th percentile:** 10 out of 100 babies are smaller, 90 are larger.
- **97th percentile:** 97 out of 100 babies are smaller.

Being on a low or high percentile does not mean something is wrong. Genetics play a big part: small parents often have small babies.

## The WHO Child Growth Standards

The World Health Organization standards, published in 2006, are based on a study of healthy, breastfed children in six countries (Brazil, Ghana, India, Norway, Oman and the United States). They describe how children *should* grow in good conditions, not just how they happen to grow.

There are separate charts for **boys and girls** and for:

- **weight-for-age**;
- **length-for-age** (lying down, up to 2 years) and height-for-age (standing, from 2 years);
- **head circumference-for-age**.

## What is normal?

- Most healthy babies are between the **3rd and 97th** percentiles.
- Babies typically **lose 5–10% of their birth weight** in the first few days, then regain it by **10–14 days**.
- Many babies move up or down a little in the first 6–8 weeks as they find their own curve.
- Growth happens in spurts, so measurements taken close together can look uneven.

## When to talk to a professional

- A baby’s weight **crosses two or more major percentile lines**, up or down.
- Weight is below the 2nd–3rd percentile, or above the 98th, especially if it is moving away from length.
- Head circumference grows much faster or slower than expected.
- Weight gain stops, or your baby seems unwell, is feeding poorly or has fewer wet diapers.

## Measuring at home

Home scales and tape measures are less precise than clinic equipment. If you track at home:

- Weigh at the same time of day, ideally without clothes or diaper.
- For length, lie your baby flat with legs gently straightened — it is easier with two people.
- For head circumference, measure around the widest part: just above the eyebrows and ears, around the back of the head.

## Babies born early

For babies born before 37 weeks, health professionals usually plot growth using **corrected age** (age from the due date) until about 2 years. Ask your doctor which age to use.

Logging every measurement in a tracker lets you see the whole curve, not just the latest dot.
