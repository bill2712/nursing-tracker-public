When you are running on little sleep, it is hard to remember whether the last feed was at 2 or 3 a.m., or which side your baby fed from. A simple log takes the guesswork out — and gives you something concrete to share at check-ups.

## What is worth tracking

**Feeds** — time, length, breast or bottle, side, and amount for bottles. Useful for spotting cluster feeding, growth spurts and how long your baby goes between feeds.

**Diapers** — wet, dirty or both. In the first weeks, diaper counts are one of the best signs that your baby is getting enough milk (see [how many wet diapers](../how-many-wet-diapers/)).

**Sleep** — naps and night sleep. Helpful for finding a rhythm and noticing when a regression or illness is affecting sleep.

**Growth** — weight, length and head circumference, plotted on [WHO growth charts](../who-growth-charts-explained/).

**Health and care** — vaccines, medicines, temperature, nose clearing, oral care and anything unusual. Notes with a time stamp are invaluable if your baby is unwell.

**Development** — new skills as you notice them, without turning them into a test.

## How a log helps at appointments

Midwives, health visitors and doctors often ask:

- How many feeds in 24 hours, and how long do they last?
- How many wet and dirty diapers?
- How much does your baby drink from a bottle?
- When did the symptoms start?

A week of entries — or a CSV export — answers all of these at a glance.

## Tips to keep tracking quick

- **One tap per event.** Use quick buttons for diapers and care tasks, and timers for feeds and sleep.
- **Log afterwards if you need to.** Forgot to press start? Add the minutes later or enter the feed afterwards.
- **Share the job.** Take turns with a partner or carer.
- **Back up regularly.** If your tracker stores data only on your phone, download a backup every week or two.
- **Know when to stop.** Once feeding is going well and your baby is growing steadily, it is fine to track less.
