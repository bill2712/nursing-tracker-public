How this calculator works

  • Most formula-fed babies under 6 months drink about 150 ml of milk per kilogram of body weight per day, with a normal range of roughly 120–180 ml/kg.
  • The daily total is divided by the number of feeds. Newborns usually feed 8–12 times in 24 hours; by 3–4 months many babies take 5–6 larger feeds.
  • Babies rarely need more than about 1,000 ml (32 oz) of formula a day. If your baby regularly wants much more or much less, talk to your health visitor or doctor.
  • After 6 months, solid food gradually replaces some milk. Around 500–600 ml of milk a day alongside solids is typical at 7–12 months.

Based on the common guideline of about 150 ml per kg per day (2.5 oz per lb) for formula-fed babies up to around 6 months, capped at about 1,000 ml (32 oz) per day. From 6 months, as solids increase, milk needs fall gradually. Breastfed babies should be fed on demand — follow your baby’s hunger and fullness cues.

Hunger and fullness cues

  • Hungry: rooting, sucking on hands, lip smacking, getting restless. Crying is a late hunger sign.
  • Full: slowing down, turning away, closing lips, relaxed hands, falling asleep.

Medical disclaimer

This guide is general information, not medical advice. Every baby is different. If you are worried, contact your doctor, midwife or health visitor. Seek urgent help if your baby has trouble breathing, is unusually sleepy or floppy, or is under 3 months old with a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher.
